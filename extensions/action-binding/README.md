This extension is for a decision that authorises an external action, such as a payment or a message to a third party. It says how to keep evidence that the action carried out is the action that was approved, and how a check of that evidence reports what it found. It adds two objects beside the decision record: an execution-evidence attachment and a verification report. It does not add a field to a decision record, and it does not change one.

Version 0.2.0 changed 0.1.0 after review: a change must say how it was given (`method`), a report must give each attachment's digest, its reviews and its executed specifications, and the check stops one approval from paying for more requests than it states. Version 0.3.0 chooses the issuer's signature, which 0.2.0 left open: it is the [signatures](../signatures/) profile's organisation seal of kind `attachment`, carried as its copy in `issuer_signature`, so that a signature cannot be moved to another attachment or made under another key reference, profile or version. The list of what moves, and what to do with 0.2.0 documents, is under "Changes from 0.2.0". After review, 0.3.0 also records a standing approval: a person approves bounds once, and requests inside them need no review of each; the check verifies that each request stayed inside, and says what that does not show ("Standing approvals"). It is an addition, with nothing newly required, so the version stays 0.3.0.

## Scope, and what this extension does not claim

- The extension is optional. Its MUST, SHALL and SHOULD sentences bind only an implementation that says it uses action binding 0.3.0.
- It does not change what a core field means or when it is required. Where this text and the Standard's text disagree, the Standard's text governs.
- It says nothing about what any law requires. It sets no rule on how many people review an action, and no expected rate of rejections.
- The records and the attachment remain input, not evidence. A matching digest shows that two stages name the same bytes. It does not show that the reviewer read them, that the action was right, or that the named person acted. A verified issuer signature shows who issued the attachment's bytes, not that what they record is any of these.
- Using this extension makes no one certified or compliant. The outcome value `approved` records only the reviewer's own act, not approval by any regulator or other authority.

## Core fields it reads

| Core field | In the Standard | What the extension reads it for |
|---|---|---|
| `decision_id` | §6.2.1 | The record the attachment sits beside; the identifier the issuer's signature signs |
| `drafting_authority` | §6.2 | Who proposed the action, when an AI worker drafted it |
| `review_log` | §5.1(2), §6.2 | The reviewer, the time and the outcome the record states |
| `affirmation_record` | §5.1(3), §5.2, §6.2 | The actor, time and method of an approval given by affirmation. They are the issuer's statements |

The attachment refers to the record by `decision_id`. It is never inserted into the record. An action is usually carried out after the record is sealed, so the record's seal does not cover the attachment. There is one attachment per decision record. It is not sealed by the Core's seal or chained: the digest that a verification report gives for it fixes which bytes were checked, and the issuer MAY sign it (item 7 below). A document this extension reads that names a member twice, at any depth, is not read (item 8).

## The attachment

An attachment made under this extension:

1. MUST identify the profile and its version (`dps-action-binding`, `0.3.0`) and the record it sits beside.
2. MUST carry each action specification that is proposed, shown, approved, requested or attempted, with the SHA-256 digest of its exact bytes. The specification holds what is material: the operation, the target, the parameters, the input-version references and the applicable policy version. The deployer decides what else is material. Anything left out of the specification is not bound by the approval.
3. MUST record these as separate stages, each with its own id and the issuer's time: the proposal (by an AI worker or a person), the review (with the digest of the specification shown, or of the bounds shown), the outcome (`approved`, `rejected` or `escalated`), a standing approval and a revocation of it where there is one, the authorised request, each recheck of the authorisation, each execution attempt, and each observed result. A review MAY name the proposal it answers (`proposal_ref`). A review shown something other than its proposal names who edited it (`edited_by`): that person is the author of the change, and the report shows them.
4. MUST bind an approval to the digest shown in the review it answers. A review has one outcome, given by the person who was shown the specification. An outcome MAY name the core field that records the same act (`core_ref`): the `affirmation_record` or a `review_log` entry. A core field backs at most one outcome. A rejection or an escalation carries no approved digest, and MUST NOT be represented as an approval.
5. MUST record the authority that an approval asserts. An approval covers one request unless it states a `request_limit`, and a change of it counts as one of those requests. However many approvals and changes bind one specification, the requests for it number at most the largest `request_limit` any of them states. A standing approval states its count and its period inside its bounds, under their digest, and never a `request_limit`.
6. MUST NOT let an approval carry over to a changed specification. Any change gives a new digest. The new digest needs a renewed review and approval, or a separately authorised change that names the earlier approval and a different specification. A change is authorised by a named person, who states their own authority and how they gave it: never by the proposer, by a service named in the attachment, or by default. A change cannot reach a specification that a reviewer in the attachment rejected or escalated: overriding that needs a new review. No reviewer is shown a specification reached only through a change, and the report says so. A change never starts from a standing approval. Where a change alters what the affirmed record decided, the Standard's rule applies: a correction is a new record that supersedes it (§5.1(3)).
7. MUST, where the issuer signs it, carry the signature as the copy of the signatures 0.2.0 organisation seal of kind `attachment`, in `issuer_signature`: exactly five members, `algorithm` exactly `Ed25519`, `key_ref`, `signed_bytes_ref` exactly the text `dps-signatures 0.2.0 organisation_seal`, `sealed_at`, and `value_b64`. The signed bytes are that profile's, under anchoring 0.2.0's shared rule: `dps-signatures`, `0.2.0`, `organisation_seal`, the key reference, `attachment`, the attachment's `decision_id`, the sealed digest, and `sealed_at`. The sealed digest is the SHA-256 of the attachment's bytes as issued with every character of `issuer_signature.value_b64` replaced by `A`, exactly as the signatures profile has it, and nothing else replaced: `sealed_at` is written in place, and is therefore under the digest as well as in the signed bytes. The verifier builds the signed bytes from the attachment itself (its `decision_id`, its bytes as received with the replacement, the time written in the copy), so a copy moved onto another attachment, made over the bytes with the value present, or made before a stage was added fails by construction, and nothing in the attachment has to be kept in agreement with the signature. Anything other than these exact values fails, as the signatures profile fails a copy that names other values. The seal MAY also be carried in a signatures attachment beside this one; the two then agree member for member, and that profile's check reports it (`embedded_copy`). An attachment MAY be unsigned, as before.
8. A document this extension reads that names a member twice, at any depth, is not read, as I-JSON (RFC 7493 §2.3) has it: a reader keeping the last member and one keeping the first would read two different attachments under one signature, and only the issuer can write that. The reference check reads every document so: its cases, its vectors, the schemas, the records and the specifications' bytes.

## Standing approvals

An organisation may approve once that requests within bounds may be made without a review of each: "create a task on this board, title of at most 80 characters, at most 200 times, until 31 December". Under items 3 to 6 alone, each run has its own specification and so its own digest, and reads as executed with no approval. A standing approval records what happened instead: a named person approved the bounds, and each request stayed inside them.

9. A standing approval (`standing_approval`) MUST answer a review that was shown the bounds' digest (`shown_bounds_digest`), as an approval answers a review shown a specification's; it is given by the person shown the bounds, who states their authority and how they gave it. Never the proposer, a service named in the attachment, or a default. The bounds travel in the stage as exact bytes under `bounds_digest`, which is the digest the review was shown. An outcome other than `approved` MAY answer a review of bounds; an approval of bounds is never an outcome.
10. The bounds are a JSON object with exactly these members, read as the exact bytes say and nothing else: `operation`, `target` and `policy_version`, each the exact value a specification must have; `input_version_refs`, the references a specification may name; `parameters`, one constraint for every parameter a specification may carry; `max_requests`; `valid_from` and `valid_until`, times with an offset. A constraint is one of four: `{"exact": v}`, `{"one_of": [v, …]}` (distinct values), `{"max_length": n}` (a string, its length in Unicode code points), or `{"min": a}`, `{"max": b}` or both (a JSON integer). A JSON integer here is one from -(2^53-1) to 2^53-1, as I-JSON (RFC 7493 §2.2) has it; any other number is not one: a reader using doubles would round a larger one, and a number with a fraction or an exponent is never one. Values are strings, JSON integers, booleans or null, so `1` and `1.0` are not the same value, nor `1` and `true`. The bounds are closed: a member or a parameter the bounds do not name puts a specification outside them, and so does a parameter the bounds name that the specification lacks. Nothing in the bounds is a pattern, a format, or a fraction: two independent checks must reach one verdict, and these four kinds leave no room for two.
11. A request MAY rest on a standing approval (`authorised_by`). The check then compares the specification's bytes with the bounds: the three exact members, every input-version reference allowed, every parameter inside its constraint; the request's time inside the period; the requests recorded as resting on the approval at most `max_requests`; no request listed or dated after a recorded revocation of it; and no specification a reviewer in the attachment rejected or escalated. The executor rechecks the authorisation before each attempt, as for any request: the live state of a standing approval comes from that recheck, not from this record.
12. A revocation (`standing_revocation`) is the issuer's record that the approval was withdrawn, by a named person who says how. A request after it is not authorised. Its absence shows nothing. Withdrawing a person's key, in the signatures profile, and withdrawing their mandate stay separate acts.
13. Bounds whose bytes are unavailable, or cannot be read (a member named twice, a number that is not finite), leave every request resting on them unchecked: the report says so, and never that the request was inside. Bytes that do not match `bounds_digest`, or that are not well-formed bounds, fail the requests resting on them.

**What this shows, and what it does not.** That a named person approved these bounds, for this operation and target, this period and this count, and that each request the attachment records stayed inside them. The count is of requests recorded, not requests run: an export can leave some out, and an older signed attachment verifies too. The period is checked against the issuer's times; an independent timestamp over the attachment's signature bounds them from above only. "No revocation recorded" means that and nothing more. No one is shown to have looked at any run. A standing approval approves nothing in any legal sense, and the records stay input, not evidence; a specification reached only through one is reported as `via_standing_approval`, never as shown to a reviewer.

## Beside the attachment

Independent timestamps over the issuer signature's value travel in the signatures attachment read beside this one, as anchoring 0.2.0 timestamp assertions over a signature value, checked exactly as the signatures profile checks them: never inside the action-binding attachment, so that adding one later changes no signed bytes. A timestamp counts for the signature whose value its imprint covers; a subject that names another assertion fails, as that profile has it, and the timestamp then counts for nothing. The seal of kind `attachment` MAY travel there too (item 7).

## The executor

An executor that uses this extension:

- MUST execute only a specification whose digest an approval or an authorised change binds, or that is inside the bounds of a standing approval, through a request that names that approval, change or standing approval.
- MUST recheck the authorisation before each attempt, and record the recheck. If the latest recheck does not authorise the action, for example because a permission was revoked after the approval, it MUST NOT attempt it. A retry is a new attempt, with its own recheck.
- MUST give each request a `request_id` that is unique within the attachment, and MUST NOT run a second request with the same `request_id` as a new action. Where the target accepts an idempotency key, the executor sends the `request_id` as that key.
- MUST record an observed result for each attempt: `succeeded`, `failed` or `indeterminate`, with the basis of the observation. An attempt is not a completed action, and a result is never inferred from the attempt. When the outcome cannot be established, as after a timeout, the result is `indeterminate`.

## The verifier and its report

A verifier that uses this extension:

- MUST check each specification's bytes against its digest, and report unavailable bytes as unavailable. It MUST check the statements the attachment makes outside the digest against those bytes where it can read them: the operation, the target, the policy version and the input-version references in the `summary`. Where it cannot read them, it reports them as not checked. A matching digest alone does not show that a specification is the action its summary names.
- MUST check the stages' statements about core fields against the decision record, where it holds the record: the `decision_id`, an AI proposer against `drafting_authority`, and an outcome against the `affirmation_record` or the `review_log` entry it names. It reports them as verified only when every approval names a core field and every statement matches. An approval that names no core field is reported as not checked, because the record does not show it. So is a `review_log` outcome word the check does not know, a core time with no offset, and a `review_log` index not written as an integer. A matching `decision_id` shows consistency only, since a verifier usually finds the record by that same id. Without the record, it reports these statements as not checked.
- MUST check that the shown, approved, requested and attempted digests agree, or are joined by an authorised change; that each review has one outcome, and each core field backs at most one; that a change names a different specification, uses up one request of what it changes, is not authorised by the proposer or by a service named in the attachment, and does not reach a specification a reviewer rejected or escalated; that a review shown something other than its proposal names who edited it; that each stage comes after the stages it names; that no request rests on a rejection or an escalation; that no `request_id` is used twice, no approval or change covers more requests than it states, and no specification is requested more often than the largest `request_limit` of the approvals and changes that bind it; that each attempt follows an authorising recheck; and that each attempt has exactly one result. For a standing approval, it MUST check the bounds' bytes against their digest and read them as item 10 says, and each request resting on them as item 11 says; it reports the requests as not checked, never as inside, where the bytes are unavailable or cannot be read, and fails them where the bytes are not the digest's or not well-formed bounds. A recheck that authorised a request after a recorded revocation is reported against the recheck. Times are the issuer's, and the report says so. They are compared as instants: `09:12:00Z` and `09:12:00+00:00` are the same time. A time the schema's pattern accepts but no calendar has, a date that does not exist such as `2026-02-30` or a leap second (`23:59:60`), names no instant: each check that compares it fails, with a finding naming it, and the check does not stop.
- MUST take issuer trust from a trust policy it configured independently, never from the attachment. The policy is anchoring 0.2.0's (`dps-anchoring-trust-policy`, version `0.2.0`, schema [`../anchoring/schemas/trust-policy.schema.json`](../anchoring/schemas/trust-policy.schema.json)): the issuer's key is in role `organisation`, and the key states are anchoring's, by reference, with the copy's `sealed_at` as the stated time. A compromised key counts only with a verified independent timestamp earlier than `revoked_at` over the signature value; with none, the time cannot be established. The verifier MUST refuse a policy that lists one public key under two references, holders or roles, or one reference to two entries, and report the refusal once (`trust_policy`) and consistently: every issuer signature, key state and timestamp that relies on the policy fails with the reason. A verifier holding only unsigned attachments still names the policy it would use and says whether it accepts it; with nothing signed, that status decides nothing else in the report.
- MUST check a signed attachment's `issuer_signature` as the signatures profile checks a seal: build the signed bytes from the attachment, verify them with the policy's key for `key_ref`, in role `organisation`, with the pinned certificate holding the key when there is one, and report the key's state when it signed. It MUST report the signature as one dimension (`issuer_signature`), verified only when the signature and the key's state are, naming the key reference, the algorithm, where the key came from (only ever `trust_policy`), the certificate check, the key state with what its time rests on, and the **holder the policy gives for the key**: the attachment names no issuer, so a signature made with another organisation's key that the policy also lists verifies as that organisation's, and the reader needs to see whose. `certificate` means, by reference to the signatures profile, that the certificate the policy pins for the key holds it, with no chain to a trusted list and no revocation check; `not_checked` means that none is pinned. The dimension keeps `attests: issuer_recorded_claims_only`. An unsigned attachment is reported `not_checked`, with the finding that it carries no signature; it is never reported as verified. A copy whose `sealed_at` is earlier than the latest stage the attachment records is reported with a finding, both being the issuer's times; the signature stands.
- MUST give exactly one result for each attachment, with its index, its `decision_id` and its `attachment_digest` (the SHA-256 of the attachment's bytes as received), and state the attachment count. No two results name the same `decision_id`; this can be checked only among the attachments one report covers. The `attachment_digest` fixes the bytes for a reader who holds the report as the verifier issued it. Within each result it gives one result for each specification. It says, for each review, whether the reviewer was shown what was proposed and, if not, who edited it; and, for each executed specification, whether a reviewer was shown it, it was reached only through a change, or only through a request under a standing approval. Where the attachment records a standing approval, it gives one entry for each (`standing_approvals`): the bounds as read, the requests recorded, the count and the period the bounds state where they were read, whether a revocation is recorded, and what none of this shows. It carries through the observed result of each attempt and its basis, and reports a missing result as missing. Where a signatures attachment is read beside the attachments, it names that attachment's digest (`signatures_attachment_digest`). JSON Schema cannot compare two documents, so a reader holding both checks this; the test runner does.
- MUST report time as verified only against a verified independent timestamp over this attachment's verified issuer signature, from the attachment beside, and say exactly what it gives: the signature, and so the attachment's bytes as issued, existed no later than T (`no_later_than`, the token's time with its accuracy), by the authority named (`source_ref`) and the timestamp it rests on (`timestamp_index`). Not when any stage happened. A timestamp over a value that does not verify as this attachment's signature shows nothing about it. Where several cover it, the earliest gives the time. A stage the attachment dates after that time is reported with a finding: the issuer's stage times are inconsistent with the timestamp, and the time stands.
- MUST report each check as a separate dimension, and MUST NOT merge them into one pass, fail or compliance result. It keeps `not_checked`, `unavailable` and `not_supported` explicit, and states the scope of its conclusion. That scope never includes what the reviewer actually saw: the report shows only which digest the attachment says was shown. A report may say less than the reference check (a signature it did not check); it may not say more.

## Separate assertions

Four statements are reported separately. None stands for another.

- **Human identity.** A stage's `actor_ref` is the issuer's statement of who acted. The report records identity as verified only against an independent source it names. An organisation's seal binds no person.
- **Organisational authority.** The authority an approval asserts. The report records it as verified only against an independent source, such as the permission system the executor rechecked. The recheck stage is itself the issuer's record, so the verifier asks the permission system itself. An organisation's seal grants no authority.
- **Issuer signature.** The copy of the organisation's seal (item 7). **What a verified one shows:** the holder the verifier's own trust policy names for the key issued these exact bytes, under this `decision_id`, at a time the key was in use. Where no independent timestamp covers the signature, that time is the issuer's own `sealed_at`: a key listed only as superseded passes on it, so whoever holds a leaked key that the issuer lists only as superseded can backdate; a key that may have leaked is marked compromised, and then only a timestamp earlier than the compromise counts. **What it does not show:** that the named person reviewed anything; that the reviewer saw what the attachment says was shown; that the action was right, or that the claims are true; when any stage happened, or that the stage times are true; that no stage was left out before signing; or that this is the issuer's latest attachment for the decision, since an older, validly signed attachment with fewer stages verifies as well. Anyone holding a signed attachment can remove `issuer_signature` and present it as unsigned: with the seal carried beside, that seal then fails; with nothing beside, no check can see it. A relying party that needs a signed attachment requires one in its own configuration, and does not accept an unsigned one.
- **Legal signature.** An outcome may reference one (`legal_signature_ref`). Whether a signature has legal effect is outside this extension.

## Rationale provenance

Each outcome states when its rationale was recorded: `contemporaneous` (with the outcome, no later than its time), `later_supplemented`, or `not_recorded`. A recorded rationale also states whether it was drafted with AI assistance (`ai_assisted`). A later explanation MUST NOT be presented as contemporaneous. A verifier MUST compare the stated timing with the issuer's times. It cannot tell who wrote the text.

## Decisions and telemetry

The Core is not a real-time telemetry format, and it lets records reference traces kept elsewhere (§5.3). This extension keeps that line. The attachment records the stages of one reviewed action. Detailed machine traces MAY stay in another system, referenced by `trace_refs`. They MUST NOT be copied into the attachment, which is not a stream of machine events.

## Relation to the other extensions, and dependencies

This extension reuses two definitions from [portable verification](../portable-verification/) 0.3.0, by reference, and applies them to action specifications instead of records: exact bytes (item 3 of its export rules: the bytes that were hashed, unchanged) and the representation rule (item 5: the original bytes, or a canonical form named with a specification that fixes every byte); these items are the same in 0.1.0, 0.2.0 and 0.3.0. Both use SHA-256 in lowercase hex. Using action binding does not require using portable verification. A record exported under portable verification and its attachment are checked side by side, each under its own extension.

For a signed attachment, this version rests on two Proposals, each by exact version: signatures 0.2.0 (the organisation seal, its signed bytes, the replacement rule, the check of a signature, of a seal and of a timestamp) and anchoring 0.2.0 (the shared signed-bytes rule, the trust policy, the key states, the timestamp assertion). Unsigned use needs neither for its checks. The Core, the stages and the binding checks do not change. The reference check in `tests/run_checks.py` loads the signatures extension's own runner for the signature, key-state and timestamp checks, so that the two cannot drift; it needs that runner's requirements (`tests/requirements.txt`).

A stage time or a rationale's time written with a leap second or as 2026-02-30, in A0, A1 and A2 (a request, and a revocation), is reported failed in each check that compares it, and the check does not stop.
- **Signature cases** run the reference check of one attachment's issuer signature. They verify A0 with its pinned certificate and the timestamp beside, A0 on the stated time, the same record's older attachment with fewer stages (a limitation case: nothing says it is not the latest), and B0 as the signatures vectors verify it; an unsigned attachment is not checked. They detect the copy moved onto another attachment, a stage added or an outcome changed after signing, the value replaced by another character than `A`, `sealed_at` changed after signing, a key the policy does not list, a person's or a witness key, another profile, version or kind, another key reference, a changed byte, a superseded key used after `revoked_at` or with a timestamp after it, a compromised key with no timestamp, one earlier than the compromise and one later, a refused policy, a second organisation's key (verified, the report naming that holder), a `sealed_at` that is not a real time or before the key's validity, a copy whose `sealed_at` is not the seal's beside, the signature removed with nothing beside (a limitation case), and a `sealed_at` earlier than the latest stage. Timestamps naming another attachment, from an unlisted authority, or over a signature that does not verify do not count; one earlier than the stated time does. A verified timestamp over another attachment's signature, named like A0's, gives A0 no time and gives the other its own; with two covering A0's signature, the earliest gives the time; and a stage dated after that time is a finding. A stage dated `9999-12-31T23:00:00-05:00`, a real instant whose UTC date falls after the year 9999, gives both findings, printed as the attachment writes the time.
- **Pairing cases** read the report with its attachments, and for A2 detect bounds the reference check could not read reported as verified, a specification reached through a standing approval reported as shown to a reviewer, a recorded revocation reported as none, and three requests reported as two, while accepting a report that leaves the standing approvals out; the records, the attachment beside and the policy. They detect a missing, duplicated or relabelled result, a wrong count, two attachments for the same decision record, an attachment changed after the report was made, a specification without a result, an observed result, its basis or the execution state not carried through as recorded, a specification reached only through a change reported as shown to a reviewer, the editor of a proposal not carried through, a dimension reported as verified that the reference check does not verify, an unsigned attachment or an unlisted key's signature reported verified, a holder that is not the policy's, time or a key state resting on a timestamp that was not read, time verified on a timestamp over a signature that does not verify, a removed signature reported verified, a refused policy reported accepted, and an attachment digest beside that is not the document's; a verified time with another time, authority or timestamp than the reference check finds, an accepted policy reported as refused, a report resting its time on the later of two timestamps, and a report, otherwise right, that gives A0 the time of a timestamp over another record's signature; and they accept a report that says less than it could, a changed attachment reported as it is, and the reference report itself. A second standing approval with no request resting on it and its bounds unavailable leaves the binding verified, and the schema accepts that report; the entry's five limits are stated in the reference words.

## Changes from 0.2.0

**Added after review, within 0.3.0 (standing approvals, [#72](https://github.com/decision-provenance-standard/standard/issues/72)):** two stage kinds, `standing_approval` and `standing_revocation`; a review may be shown bounds (`shown_bounds_digest`) instead of a specification; a request may rest on a standing approval; a fourth `reached_by` value, `via_standing_approval`; a `standing_approvals` entry in a result; and bounds written with a number that is not finite (`1e400`) are not read, so the requests resting on them are not checked (item 13). The shared strict reader is unchanged: every document it read before reads the same. Nothing is newly required: every attachment and report valid before is valid now, with the same meaning, and the check's verdicts on them do not change. By the rule agreed on #28, the version stays 0.3.0; a member that becomes required moves it to 0.4.0.

**Fixed after merge, within 0.3.0:** a stage time or a rationale's time that the schema's pattern accepts but that names no instant (a leap second, or a date such as 2026-02-30) stopped the reference check with an error, for A0 and A1 as well. It is now reported: each check that compares the time fails, with a finding naming it, as anchoring and signatures report such a time. No document changes validity, and the verdicts on every time that names an instant are unchanged, so the version stays 0.3.0. A stage time that is a real instant but whose UTC date falls after the year 9999 or before the year 1, such as `9999-12-31T23:00:00-05:00`, stopped the reference report where a finding printed it in UTC; such a finding now prints it as the attachment writes it. The time is compared as an instant, as before, and no verdict or finding on any other time changes.

For anyone who implemented the 0.2.0 text (#21, as it stood at `2fead0c`). The rule agreed on #28 asks whether a change makes documents valid under the published text invalid; the suite does, so the version moves.

**What 0.2.0 allowed and 0.3.0 refuses:**

- A signed attachment whose `issuer_signature` was made another way: another suite, other signed bytes, no `sealed_at`, a member a copy does not have. 0.2.0 said that the signed bytes SHOULD include the profile, its version and the key reference, and that the SHOULD would become a MUST once a suite was chosen (#52). It is chosen: the signatures 0.2.0 organisation seal of kind `attachment`, `algorithm` and `signed_bytes_ref` as fixed values (a 0.2.0 copy labelled `EdDSA` was valid), `sealed_at` required, written in place and signed. An attachment signed otherwise fails at `issuer_signature`, and its member is not valid against the attachment schema.
- A document this extension reads that names a member twice, at any depth. It is not read.
- A report that does not say whether the trust policy was accepted; that calls `issuer_signature` verified without naming its key reference, algorithm, holder or key state, or with a key state that is not verified; that calls a compromised key verified on the stated time; that calls time verified on any basis other than a timestamp over the signature, or without saying by when the signature existed; or that names `same_attachment` as where a key came from.

**Other changes, which refuse nothing a correct 0.2.0 document stated:** timestamps over the signature value travel beside the attachment; the report's `issuer_signature` names the holder, the certificate check and the key state; `time_assurance` carries `no_later_than` and `timestamp_index`; `trust_policy` and `signatures_attachment_digest` are added; the trust policy is anchoring 0.2.0's by reference; portable verification is cited by exact version (0.2.0 when this version was written, 0.3.0 since its own 0.3.0), items 3 and 5 being the same in each. An unsigned attachment is exactly as valid as before.

**A 0.3.0 verifier and a 0.2.0 document.** The schemas pin `profile_version` to `0.3.0`, so a 0.2.0 attachment or report is not valid input to this version's check, and the check does not read it. A 0.2.0 attachment stays a 0.2.0 attachment, checked by the 0.2.0 text and check as they stood at `2fead0c`. The same unsigned content labelled `0.3.0` is a valid 0.3.0 attachment; a signed one has to be signed again under the suite. The signatures profile compares a copy by its attachment's version: four members in a 0.2.0 attachment, five in a 0.3.0 one.
